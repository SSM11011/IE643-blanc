# Deep Literature & Benchmark Review: Unified Multilingual Indian-Language ASR with Integrated LID (for Team "blanc", IIT Bombay)

## TL;DR
- **Base model:** For a single unified Transformer with integrated LID under a student compute budget, fine-tune **Whisper large-v3-turbo** (809M params — a pruned/fine-tuned large-v3 with decoder layers cut from 32→4, ~6× faster, performing comparably to large-v2) with **LoRA/QLoRA**; use **Whisper-small (244M)** as the fast prototyping/dev model. The strongest Indic-specific baseline to beat/compare is **AI4Bharat IndicConformer-600M** (MIT, all 22 languages, CTC+RNNT); the strongest 2026 open LLM-ASR alternative with built-in LID is **Qwen3-ASR-0.6B/1.7B** (Apache 2.0, 52 languages/dialects).
- **Key evidence-based design choices:** (1) Keep the Whisper **language token as a supervised target** during fine-tuning to prevent the well-documented LID collapse on low-resource languages, and add an **auxiliary encoder LID-CTC objective** (Interspeech 2025: LID-CTC on MMS-1B gave a **14% relative LID-accuracy improvement and 30% relative CER reduction**, reaching 82.4% LID / 16.3% CER). (2) Use **two-stage balanced upsampling** (Polyglot-Lion, MeLLM 2026) or **cosine-scheduled dynamic balancing** (Canary-1B-v2). (3) **Do NOT run a generic denoiser (MetricGAN+, SAM-Audio, DeepFilterNet) in front of a strong ASR model** — multiple 2025–2026 studies show it *hurts*; instead use **VAD segmentation + training-time augmentation** and **Calm-Whisper-style non-speech/blank-label data** to kill hallucinations.
- **Evaluation:** Report **CER alongside WER** (CER is more meaningful for Tamil/Malayalam and for Indic spelling variation), apply a consistent normalizer (Whisper + IndicNLP/NFC), give **per-language breakdowns + LID confusion matrix + oracle-vs-predicted-LID gap**, and benchmark on **IndicVoices, Kathbath/IndicSUPERB, Vistaar, FLEURS, Svarah, Lahaja**, plus the new **Voice of India** (2026) real-world set. Flag that vanilla Whisper large-v3 exceeds 100% WER on several Indic scripts (Bengali, Malayalam) without normalization.

---

## Key Findings

1. **The Indian-language ASR landscape is dominated by AI4Bharat (IndicConformer, IndicWhisper, IndicVoices) and NLTM/Bhashini-funded datasets.** European-centric 2025–2026 releases (Canary-1B-v2, Parakeet-TDT-0.6b-v3) cover **25 European languages and NO Indian languages** — they are excellent methodological references (data balancing, hallucination tricks) but not usable as an Indic base.
2. **Whisper's LID degrades after fine-tuning on low-resource languages** — a reproducible, community-confirmed failure. The fix is to train the language token as a target and/or add an auxiliary LID objective; this is directly aligned with the project's "integrated LID head on the encoder" deliverable.
3. **LoRA/PEFT is the correct fine-tuning approach for a student budget.** LoRA-Whisper cut multilingual WER from 11.68% → 9.52% (vs a 9.19% monolingual oracle) — an 18.5% relative gain for multilingual and 23.0% for language expansion, at ~5% of trainable params. PEFT INT8/QLoRA lets Whisper-large fine-tune on a single free T4 (<8 GB) with near-parity to full fine-tuning (Marathi: 14.01 vs 13.64 normalized WER).
4. **Generic speech enhancement before a strong ASR model is now shown to be counterproductive** in at least three independent 2025–2026 studies (medical ASR, SAM-Audio zero-shot, LLM voice pipelines). VAD + augmentation is the evidence-backed preprocessing path.
5. **Data balancing is the single highest-leverage training decision** for imbalanced tiers. Deterministic two-stage upsampling (Polyglot-Lion) and cosine-scheduled weight annealing toward uniform (Canary) both beat naive concatenation and are simpler/more robust than temperature sampling.

## 1. Latest Open Multilingual ASR Models (base/baseline candidates)

**Whisper large-v3 / large-v3-turbo (OpenAI, MIT).** Encoder-decoder, 99+ languages, native LID via `<|lang|>` token, timestamps, translation. large-v3 = 1.55B params; **large-v3-turbo = 809M** — per OpenAI's model card, "a finetuned version of a pruned Whisper large-v3" where "the number of decoding layers [was] reduced from 32 to 4," trained for two additional fine-tuning epochs and performing comparably to large-v2, ~6× faster. This is the recommended base: it satisfies "single unified Transformer with shared representations + built-in LID." Caveat: vanilla Whisper is weak/broken on several Indic scripts (Bengali, Malayalam WER >100% before normalization).

**OWSM v4 (CMU/ESPnet, Interspeech 2025 Best Student Paper; peng25c).** Fully open Whisper-style model trained on **166,000 hours across 75 languages** (YODAS + a scalable cleaning pipeline). Sizes incl. medium ~1B. Significantly outperforms OWSM v3.1 on LID and multilingual ASR; matches/surpasses Whisper and MMS in several scenarios. Uses 8× subsampling (80 ms encoder resolution). **OWSM-CTC** (ACL 2024) is a non-autoregressive CTC variant with LID support. Value here: fully reproducible recipes (ESPnet `egs2/owsm_v4`), strong academic reference; Indic coverage weaker than AI4Bharat.

**Qwen3-ASR-0.6B / 1.7B (Alibaba/Qwen, 29 Jan 2026; arXiv 2601.21337, Apache 2.0).** LLM-based (built on Qwen3-Omni), **all-in-one LID + ASR for 52 languages and dialects** (30 languages + 22 Chinese dialects). 1.7B "achieves SOTA performance among open-sourced ASR models" and is competitive with the strongest proprietary APIs; 0.6B gives the best accuracy/efficiency trade-off — "an average TTFT as low as 92ms and transcribe 2000 seconds speech in 1 second at a concurrency of 128." Multilingual eval on Common Voice, FLEURS, MLS, MLC-SLM. The most compelling 2026 alternative base for an LLM-ASR path; **Polyglot-Lion (below) already demonstrates a balanced fine-tune of Qwen3-ASR that includes Tamil.**

**NVIDIA Canary-1B-v2 (978M; 32 encoder/8 decoder layers) & Parakeet-TDT-0.6b-v3 (Sept 2025; arXiv 2509.14128).** FastConformer encoder + Transformer decoder (Canary) / TDT decoder (Parakeet). **25 European languages only — no Indian languages.** Trained on 1.7M hours (Granary + NeMo ASR Set 3.0). Canary beats Whisper-large-v3 on English ASR at ~10× speed. Key transferable tricks: **dynamic data balancing** (cosine schedule toward uniform language/corpus weights), **36,000 hours of non-speech audio paired with empty-string targets "to teach the model when not to generate transcriptions"** (anti-hallucination), and auto-LID. Use as methods reference, not as Indic base.

**Meta MMS-1B-all / SeamlessM4T v2 / w2v-BERT 2.0.** MMS-1B-all: wav2vec2-style encoder + lightweight per-language adapters, 1000+ languages incl. many Indic; strong on ML-SUPERB 2.0 with LoRA + mid-layer fine-tuning (2nd place, Interspeech 2025). SeamlessM4T-v2-large substantially outperforms Whisper large-v3 on several Indic FLEURS languages (Marathi 10.7 vs 24.3 CER; Bengali 9.0 vs 33.9 CER). w2v-BERT 2.0 (Conformer SSL encoder) is the backbone of the top TalTech ML-SUPERB 2.0 LID system.

**AI4Bharat IndicConformer-600M-multilingual (MIT).** The country's first open ASR covering **all 22 scheduled Indian languages**; hybrid **CTC + RNNT** Conformer; per-language and multilingual checkpoints on HF; requires the AI4Bharat NeMo fork. This is the **primary Indic baseline** to compare against. **IndicWhisper** (Whisper fine-tuned on Vistaar-train, 10.7K hours, 12 languages) has the lowest WER on 39/59 Vistaar benchmarks (avg −4.1 WER).

**Microsoft paza-whisper-large-v3-turbo (Microsoft Research Africa).** Whisper-turbo fine-tuned for low-resource (Kenyan) languages; notable for a **words-per-second (WPS) output-truncation post-processing trick** (grid search 0.5–8.0 WPS, truncate `max_words = duration × WPS`, plus repetition detection) to kill trailing hallucinations without touching weights. Directly reusable.

**Other 2025–2026 releases (context):** Distil-Whisper (English-only, 6× faster); Granite Speech 3.3 (IBM, strong English); Canary-Qwen 2.5B. Gemma 3n / Voxtral / Phi-4-multimodal / Kyutai / Moonshine are multimodal/LLM-speech systems with limited verified Indic ASR numbers — treat as watch-list, not baselines.

## 2. Whisper Fine-Tuning for Multilingual & Low-Resource ASR

- **LoRA-Whisper (Song et al., Interspeech 2024; arXiv 2406.06619):** LoRA matrices per language mitigate interference; across 8 languages, multilingual WER dropped **11.68% → 9.52%** (near the 9.19% monolingual oracle) — **18.5% relative (multilingual) and 23.0% (language expansion)** at ~5% trainable params. LoRA-MoE and "LoRA warm start" (init a new-language LoRA from the most similar existing language via Whisper's LID) add ~5%. No confirmed official code repo; third-party reimplementations exist.
- **Towards Rehearsal-Free Multilingual ASR (Xu et al., Interspeech 2024):** O-LoRA / O-AdaLoRA for adding low-resource languages (Uyghur, Tibetan) without forgetting.
- **PEFT/QLoRA recipes (HF PEFT repo):** INT8 + LoRA fine-tunes Whisper-large on <8 GB VRAM (free Colab T4). Marathi: full FT 13.64 vs PEFT 14.01 normalized WER — near-parity. Canonical scripts: HF "Fine-Tune Whisper" blog (full FT) and `Vaibhavs10/fast-whisper-finetuning` + `huggingface/peft` int8 Whisper notebook.
- **Weighted cross-entropy for low-resource languages (Piñeiro-Martín et al., Interspeech 2024; arXiv 2409.16954):** up-weight low-resource tokens in CE; single multilingual+multitask fine-tune of Whisper-small (244M) with the language token included in labels so LID is learned jointly. Hardware: single 24 GB GPU, 8,000 steps, batch 16, LR 1e-5.
- **Optuna-LoRA (Yang et al.):** Optuna HPO on LoRA gives 6–21% relative WER reduction over untuned LoRA (Uyghur/Kazakh/Kyrgyz).
- **Layer freezing / encoder-freezing:** CKA analysis shows lower encoder layers are language-independent; **freezing the bottom 10–20 layers (~33% for whisper-small) improves WER 4–6% relative** on FLEURS and reduces overfitting.
- **Multistage fine-tuning + long-form data generation** (Swiss German, arXiv 2412.15726) mitigates catastrophic forgetting and preserves segmentation; note Whisper can hallucinate domain-specific strings (e.g., subtitle credits).
- **Task arithmetic with support languages (Rafkin et al., 2026; arXiv 2601.07038):** fine-tune Whisper-tiny per language → task vectors → linear merge with a high-resource "support" language optimized on target-language val WER. Training-free composition; relevant for the low-resource tier (Maithili/Konkani/Santali) by borrowing from Hindi/Bengali.
- **Zipper-LoRA / HDMoLE / mixture-of-LoRA-experts:** rank-level decoupling of shared vs language-specific subspaces and dynamic-routing MoE-LoRA address the stability-plasticity dilemma under imbalance. The MLC-SLM 2025 winner (TEA-ASLP, 9.60% WER, 1st) used a **multilingual MoE-LoRA + known-LID + CTC-token prompts** on top of Ideal-LLM.
- **Overcoming Decoder Inconsistencies in Whisper for Dravidian languages (arXiv 2606.09535):** self-conditioning + auxiliary supervision + adaptive attention weighting; Whisper-medium, 4×A100, 3 epochs, AdamW, batch 16, LR 1e-5 (5e-5 for new params).

**Recommendation under limited GPU:** QLoRA on Whisper-small for prototyping (fits Kaggle/Colab T4), then LoRA (or QLoRA) on large-v3-turbo for the final model; freeze lower encoder layers; add per-tier LoRA or a small MoE-LoRA if interference appears.

## 3. Language Identification Integrated with ASR

- **The core problem:** Whisper's LID accuracy drops on low-resource languages after ASR fine-tuning (confirmed in openai/whisper Discussion #1454 and multiple papers). **Fix:** include the `<|lang|>` token in the training targets so LID is learned jointly (Piñeiro-Martín et al. report high LID accuracy learned "for free"), and/or add an explicit auxiliary head.
- **LID-aware CTC (Wang, Sun, Peng & Watanabe, Interspeech 2025; arXiv 2505.24200):** adding an **LID-CTC loss** + data augmentation to MMS-1B gave **a 14% relative LID-accuracy improvement and a 30% relative CER reduction** over baseline (reaching 82.4% LID / 16.3% CER on the standard set), earning **2nd place in the Interspeech 2025 ML-SUPERB 2.0 Challenge**. The most directly citable evidence for the project's "auxiliary LID head + joint multi-task loss" deliverable. It also found MMS-1B > XEUS > OWSM-CTC under LoRA + mid-layer fine-tuning.
- **Joint DID+ASR for Indian languages (Kumar et al., Interspeech 2025; arXiv 2607.02862):** Bottleneck encoder (acoustic) + RoBERTa encoder on CTC embeddings + gating fusion; **81.63% dialect-ID accuracy, 4.65% CER / 17.73% WER across 8 Indian languages, 33 dialects** (RESPIN corpus). Key insights: **detach DID embeddings from the ASR gradient graph** and **do not prepend dialect tokens to text** — prepending makes ASR learn false context when the predicted ID is wrong (incorrectly classified utterances showed CER 6.01%/WER 21.85% vs 4.51%/17.43% for correct). Code: `labspire/respin_did_interspeech25` (ESPnet).
- **External LID baselines:** **SpeechBrain VoxLingua107 ECAPA-TDNN** (`speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa`, 107 languages) for a frame/utterance-level baseline; **TalTech ML-SUPERB 2.0 system** (w2v-BERT 2.0/SeamlessM4T encoder + multi-resolution attention pooling) won LID at **86.8% LID accuracy, 27.4% mean CER** across 154 languages via LID→language-specific-ASR routing. Critically, the **oracle-vs-predicted-LID gap** was ~4 CER points (dev mean CER 7.9% oracle vs 11.7% predicted; 13.6% vs 18.5% dialectal) — quantifying the cost of routing errors.
- **IndicSUPERB LID:** fine-tuned models closed a large gap (reported 76% relative) on the LID task for 12 Indian languages — a good Indic LID benchmark.

**Design takeaway:** Adopt a **hybrid** — keep the Whisper decoder language token as a supervised (utterance-level) LID target AND add an **encoder-side LID-CTC auxiliary head** for frame-level robustness. Report both oracle-LID and predicted-LID ASR to quantify routing cost.

## 4. Data Balancing & Sampling

- **Polyglot-Lion two-stage deterministic upsampling (Dang & Ngo, MeLLM/ACL 2026; arXiv 2603.16184):** Stage 1 intra-language (replicate/subsample smaller corpora to match the largest within a language); Stage 2 inter-language (each language contributes an equal share — e.g., exactly 25% each for 4 languages). **Deliberately omits language tags so the model learns LID implicitly from audio** (ideal for code-switching). Fine-tuning Qwen3-ASR-1.7B this way hit avg error 14.85 across 12 benchmarks (4 languages incl. Tamil), matching a 6× larger model (MERaLiON-2-10B at 14.32) for ~$81 on a single RTX PRO 6000 and ~20× faster inference (0.10 s/sample vs 2.02). Preprocessing: drop >30 s clips, lowercase, strip punctuation. Authors explicitly prefer this over temperature sampling (avoids the sensitive temperature hyperparameter, guarantees exact parity).
- **Canary-1B-v2 dynamic data balancing:** cosine-scheduled transition of language/corpus sampling weights toward uniform during fine-tuning — mitigates narrow-domain dominance (e.g., VoxPopuli) and catastrophic forgetting.
- **Temperature sampling (Arivazhagan et al.):** the classic baseline; still widely used but harder to tune.
- **MMS balancing / curriculum:** MMS uses adapter-per-language to sidestep balancing; a clean→noisy or high→low-resource curriculum is a simple viable alternative.

**Recommendation:** Use Polyglot-Lion two-stage upsampling across the 6 languages (equal per-language share), or Canary-style cosine annealing to uniform. Equalize per-language utterance counts across tiers (En/Hi high; Mr/Ta/Bn mid; one low) and cap any single corpus's dominance.

## 5. Code-Switching (Hinglish and Indic-English)

- **MUCS 2021 (Diwan et al., Interspeech 2021; OpenSLR SLR104):** two code-switch pairs — **Hindi-English and Bengali-English**, from spoken tutorials; sub-task 1 (6 languages Hindi/Marathi/Odia/Tamil/Telugu/Gujarati, ~600h) baseline WER 30.73%; code-switch baseline ~32.45%. Downloads: Hindi-English train 7.3 GB, Bengali-English train 3.9 GB.
- **IITG-HingCoS corpus:** 25,988 Hinglish sentences (0.58M words), 25 h matching speech (9,251 sentences, 6,542-word vocab).
- **Towards Truly Multilingual ASR: Generalizing CS-ASR to Unseen Language Pairs (Paik et al., 2026; arXiv 2606.05846):** model merging + domain generalization gives only **modest** transfer of bilingual CS ability to unseen pairs — naive merging is insufficient; CS-specific methods are needed. Realistic expectation-setting.
- **Synthetic CS data via TTS / code-mixing-guided synthesis** and **transliteration/dual-script E2E** (Kumar et al., Interspeech 2021, arXiv 2106.01400) are the practical data-augmentation levers; **model-merging for code-mixed tasks** (arXiv 2510.19782) uses task vectors + TIES.
- **Voice of India (2026)** explicitly includes Hindi-English, Tamil-English, Urdu-Hindi code-switching as an evaluation axis.

**Recommendation:** Include MUCS Hindi-English (and optionally Bengali-English) in training; generate synthetic Hinglish via TTS; evaluate CS separately. Do not expect strong zero-shot CS on unseen pairs.

## 6. Noise Robustness, Hallucination & Preprocessing

- **Speech enhancement in front of strong ASR HURTS — strong, replicated evidence:**
  - *When De-noising Hurts* (arXiv 2512.17562): MetricGAN+ denoising degraded ASR in **all 40 configs** (4 models incl. Whisper/Parakeet/Gemini × 10 conditions); noisy audio beat enhanced audio everywhere (degradations 1.1–46.6% absolute semWER). Even in clean conditions denoising added a 1.32–3.19% penalty.
  - *When Denoising Hinders* (SAM-Audio + Whisper, arXiv 2603.04710): SAM-Audio improves PSNR but consistently raises WER/CER, worse for larger Whisper — perceptual gain ≠ recognition gain (tested on noisy Bengali YouTube + English).
  - *Perceptually Better, Semantically Worse* (arXiv 2608.30348): MetricGAN+ more than doubled output-divergence vs noisy speech (0.318 vs 0.135) in LLM voice pipelines.
- **Hallucination mitigation:**
  - **Calm-Whisper (Wang, Alhmoud, Alsahly, Alqurishi & Ravanelli, Interspeech 2025; arXiv 2505.12969, pp. 3414–3418):** only **3 of 20 decoder self-attention heads (#1, #6, #11)** cause >75% of non-speech hallucinations on UrbanSound; fine-tuning just those 3 heads on non-speech audio (AudioSet/DEMAND/MUSAN) with blank labels → **>80% hallucination reduction with <0.1% WER degradation** (0.07% test-clean, 0.06% test-other; >5-token hallucinations cut 742→91). Parameter-efficient and directly reusable.
  - **Canary trick:** add non-speech audio (they used 36,000 h) with empty-string targets during training.
  - **paza-whisper WPS truncation:** post-hoc word-count truncation + repetition detection at inference.
  - **SAE/activation steering** (arXiv 2606.07473): encoder-side hidden-representation steering approaches Calm-Whisper's reduction without fine-tuning.
- **VAD & segmentation:** **Silero VAD** (default in whisper-timestamped) run before Whisper avoids silence-triggered hallucinations; VAD-based chunking beats naive 30 s windows for long/telephone audio. WhisperX uses VAD-based cut-and-merge.
- **Training-time augmentation (the right lever):** SpecAugment, speed perturbation, MUSAN/DNS noise mixing, RIR reverberation. The LID-CTC paper confirms augmentation improves few-shot robustness.

**Recommendation:** VAD (Silero) for segmentation only; **no denoiser front-end**; add MUSAN/RIR/SpecAugment/speed-perturbation augmentation and Calm-Whisper-style non-speech blank data; optionally add WPS truncation at inference. Evaluate with SNR sweeps.

## 8. Evaluation Practices

- **Normalizers:** Whisper's `BasicTextNormalizer`/English normalizer + **IndicNLP** for Indic scripts; report both raw and normalized (raw Whisper FLEURS numbers can exceed 100–287% before normalization; Hindi drops ~21.9 and Tamil ~41.5 absolute after normalization).
- **ML-SUPERB 2.0 (Chen et al., Interspeech 2025):** six metrics — mean LID accuracy, mean CER, CER of the 15 worst languages, CER std, and dialect LID accuracy + dialect CER; **24 GB VRAM inference constraint, no internet** — a realistic student-scale protocol. Best submission: +23% LID accuracy, −18% CER vs baseline (general set); +15.7% LID / −30.2% CER on accented/dialectal.
- **MLC-SLM:** Task 1 uses CER/WER per language; Task 2 uses tcpCER/tcpWER; winner TENP 9.60% avg WER/CER.
- **Presentation:** per-language + per-accent WER/CER tables, **LID confusion matrix**, oracle-vs-predicted-LID ASR gap, and qualitative examples with error typology (substitution/deletion/insertion, hallucination, script/normalization). Report parameter counts, data hours, and GPU/training cost for reproducibility.

## Recommendations for This Project

**Base model (staged):**
1. **Prototype on Whisper-small (244M) with QLoRA** on Kaggle/Colab T4 to validate the full pipeline (preprocessing → training → LID → eval) fast and cheap.
2. **Final model: Whisper large-v3-turbo (809M) + LoRA (or QLoRA)**, freezing the bottom ~1/3 of encoder layers. Best accuracy/compute trade-off; satisfies "single unified Transformer + shared representations + native LID."
3. **Compare against IndicConformer-600M (MIT, 22 langs) and Qwen3-ASR-0.6B (Apache 2.0, 52-lang LID)** as the two strongest open baselines; optionally reproduce a Polyglot-Lion-style Qwen3-ASR fine-tune as a stretch goal.

**LID integration:** Keep the Whisper language token as a supervised target AND add an **encoder-side LID-CTC auxiliary head** with a tunable multi-task loss weight (evidence: +14% relative LID / −30% relative CER). Report **oracle-vs-predicted-LID** ASR to quantify routing cost (~4 CER points in TalTech). Use SpeechBrain VoxLingua107 ECAPA as an external LID baseline.

**Data balancing:** Two-stage deterministic upsampling (Polyglot-Lion) with **equal per-language share** across the 6 languages, or Canary-style cosine annealing to uniform. Cap single-corpus dominance. Since a visible LID display is a deliverable, keep the language-token target (unlike Polyglot-Lion, which drops it).

**Noise handling:** **No denoiser front-end.** Use Silero VAD for segmentation, train-time MUSAN/RIR/SpecAugment/speed-perturbation augmentation, and Calm-Whisper-style non-speech blank-label data to suppress hallucinations. Add paza-style WPS truncation at inference. Evaluate with SNR sweeps.

**Low-resource tier (Maithili/Konkani/Santali):** Source from IndicVoices/IndicVoices-R (all cover these); use LoRA warm-start from the most similar mid-resource language and/or task-arithmetic support-language merging (borrow from Hindi/Bengali).

**Code-switching:** Add MUCS Hindi-English (SLR104) + synthetic TTS Hinglish; evaluate CS separately; set modest expectations for unseen pairs.

**Evaluation design:** WER **and** CER (CER primary for Tamil; consider for Bengali/Malayalam), Whisper+IndicNLP normalizer with NFC, per-language + per-accent tables, LID confusion matrix, qualitative error typology, and benchmarking on IndicVoices + Kathbath + FLEURS + Vistaar + Svarah + Lahaja (+ Voice of India if accessible). Follow the ML-SUPERB 2.0 metric set (mean + worst-15 + std).

**Thresholds that change the plan:** If large-v3-turbo LoRA can't fit the compute/latency budget → drop to Whisper-small or a Parakeet-style CTC head. If LID accuracy <~90% on FLEURS Indic → increase the LID loss weight or strengthen the encoder LID-CTC head. If a mid-resource language's WER stays >~40% → add more IndicVoices/Shrutilipi hours or a language-specific LoRA. If code-switch WER is unacceptable → add synthetic Hinglish and dual-script decoding.

## Caveats & Conflicts
- **Whisper Indic FLEURS numbers vary wildly with normalization and script handling** — always report metric (WER vs CER) and normalization; Bengali/Malayalam frequently exceed 100% raw. **SeamlessM4T-v2 substantially outperforms Whisper on several Indic FLEURS languages**, so Whisper is not automatically the accuracy leader for Indic despite being the recommended base for its LID + turbo speed trade-off.
- **Common Voice per-language hours:** only Bengali CV22 (1,276 total / 54 validated) was pinned to a primary source; Hindi/Marathi/Tamil figures are from older Vistaar tallies — verify live on the Common Voice languages page (CV is now at v26, 2026).
- **Canary/Parakeet v3 do NOT support Indian languages** despite being strong 2025 models — methods reference only.
- **LoRA-Whisper has no confirmed official code repo**; **Spring-INX license** is not a clear SPDX tag; some OpenSLR SLR→language mappings should be double-checked.
- **Speech-enhancement-hurts-ASR** evidence is strongest for MetricGAN+/SAM-Audio and for already-robust models; it does not prove all enhancement is harmful (ASR-aware/joint enhancement may differ) — but the default recommendation for this project is no denoiser front-end.
- The **arXiv ID for the ML-SUPERB 2.0 Challenge summary** (Chen et al.) was not fully pinned; the TalTech (2506.01458) and LID-CTC (2505.24200) system papers are confirmed.
- Full per-language WER/CER numbers for the exact 6-language target set on IndicVoices were not published in a single unified table by any one paper; the team should generate these directly from IndicConformer/IndicWhisper baselines as part of deliverable (5).
